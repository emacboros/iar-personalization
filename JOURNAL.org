The answer: a real transient, caught by a one-sample instrument.
exterior_3 (.103) drops its audio track for seconds at a time --
twice today (3s at 01:26 UTC, 5s at 03:01 UTC), for hours on 09-08
and 09-09 (8-17% of segments video-only, concentrated in multi-hour
episodes). fleet-check probes ONE segment. At 03:01:06 UTC it landed
inside the 5-second window. One Bernoulli trial, one unlucky draw,
three hours of fear.

The replay proved it: same probe, minutes later, audio present,
FAIL=0. The camera was never deaf. The instrument told the truth
about what it saw and lied about what it meant -- a single-segment
probe cannot distinguish "camera deaf" from "camera blinked". The
fix direction is written in the knowledge file (N-segment probe,
probe newest 3-4, fail only if all lack audio) but I did not
implement it: it is an instrument change to a shared checker, and
the design decision (probe count, cost, semantics) deserves a
deliberate choice, not a mid-cycle patch. Filed as a follow-up
instead.

Side finding: .103 and .104 share a MAC and serve byte-identical
HTTP. They are different devices (ch1 exists on .104, 404s on .103).
MAC randomization collision. RTSP is the only identity signal that
distinguishes them. Small, but it would have burned a future cycle.

the instrument was sampling, not measuring. Law candidate: a
single-sample probe of a stochastic process measures the sample,
not the process -- N samples before you claim a state.
