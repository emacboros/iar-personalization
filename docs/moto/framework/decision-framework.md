# The Decision Framework

Built across four sessions (Aug 28-31, 2026), each rule earned by killing a previous wrong assumption. This is law. When a proposal violates it, name the violation before discussing the proposal.

## Rules

1. **Toy criterion.** The bike must be "a toy that also happens to be my vehicle." Joy is a first-class requirement. This is not vanity -- it is the honest recognition that he rides for pleasure and a bike that doesn't pull him out of the garage will rot there.

2. **Fun-to-ride > fun-to-look.** Aesthetics may inform but never carry a decision. The Calibro Bagger died on this rule: it was beautiful and it was doing illegitimate work in the decision. Revised from the earlier "aesthetic veto" which was too blunt.

3. **One bike.** The two-bike strategy (keep Svartpilen for gravel, add highway bike) died: "I'll prefer one and the Svart rots." One bike, chosen honestly; the Svartpilen stays as garage sentiment.

4. **Decide for the 49 weeks, not the 3.** Ruta 40 touring is 3 weeks/year of aspiration. The bike must serve the 49 weeks of actual use: commute + weekend riding. Most bikes can be adapted for travel later (luggage, wind protection).

5. **5-year ownership horizon.** Not a forever bike. Resale, parts, and service reality matter; romance doesn't.

6. **Primary use:** 40km highway commute 3x/week (possibly 5x) + weekend riding. Expanding deliberately into off-road as of 2026-08-31.

7. **Budget ceiling: 20M ARS** (~13.3k USD at 1500 ARS/USD). Includes gear. Written quotes only; Argentine list prices are fiction. Financing: compare total installments vs cash price, in writing.

8. **Weird-bike recognition test:** "Can I identify my bike from across the parking lot?" A bike he can't distinguish from the crowd won't hold his love.

9. **Test protocol:** ride multiple bikes back-to-back before the decisive test ride. His single-bike baseline means he has no reference for "normal." Comparison is the only instrument that measures it.

## Purchase challenge checklist (run every proposal)

- What does it solve? (If nothing, it's GAS)
- What does it cost? (Total: price + gear + insurance + first-year service)
- What does it delay? (Budget timeline, training timeline)
- Does it pass the framework? (Name any violation)
- Is research still changing the decision? If not: "You have enough. Go ride."
