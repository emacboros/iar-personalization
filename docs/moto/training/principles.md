# Training Principles (the why -- the plan itself lives in tasks/moto/)

## What off-road actually demands

Not an endurance sport in the running sense. Three systems, none built by hypertrophy training:

1. **Aerobic base.** Trail riding runs HR 130-160 for hours, in gear, in heat. Zone 2 builds mitochondrial density and capillarization. Dose-gated by time: 8-12 weeks minimum, cannot be sprinted. This is the foundation.

2. **Isometric strength-endurance.** The specific limiter. Standing on pegs = sustained sub-maximal quarter-squat; arm pump = sustained grip contraction occluding blood flow. A 200kg deadlifter gets arm pump in 20 minutes on his first trail day. Adapts fast (4-6 weeks). Wall sits = attack-position simulator. Dead hangs, farmer carries (long and moderate, not heavy).

3. **Repeated-effort capacity.** Technical sections are bursts: effort, recover, effort. Intervals cover this from week 5-6.

## Zone 2 discipline (the lifter's trap)

He spent years associating progress with proximity to failure. Endurance base is the opposite: progress comes from volume accumulated BELOW discomfort. If he can't speak full sentences, he's not in zone 2. Ex-lifters run easy sessions too hard -- junk intensity: too hard to build base, too easy to build VO2max. Going slow enough is the discipline. It will feel insultingly easy. That's correct.

## His constraints and assets

- **Sprint pattern:** adaptation doesn't sprint. Never miss twice. Deload every 4th week (volume -30%) -- he'll feel like slacking; he's absorbing.
- **100kg ex-lifter running:** shin splints are the classic 3-week trap. Running ramps slower than motivation wants. Walk-run first. Any shin/knee niggle: swap to bicycle, no ego.
- **Bicycle:** the workhorse. Low impact at his weight, high volume tolerance, leg endurance transfers. Unused -- tune-up week one (tires, brakes, chain, seat height).
- **Rucking:** closest thing to standing on pegs without a motorcycle. Sierras = gym. Also literal terrain reconnaissance.
- **Home gym:** dumbbells + machine. Strength maintenance = 1/3 of old volume, indefinitely. Machine leg press = quad-endurance tool (high-rep sets).
- **Recomposition, not a cut:** at 100kg he's the heaviest component on the bike; every kilogram is carried by his quads for hours on the pegs. Let endurance work do its recomposition side-effect. A deficit sabotages adaptation.

## Phase logic

Phase 1 Base (wk 1-6): aerobic foundation + isometrics daily-ish. Exit: 90min continuous zone 2 conversational.
Phase 2 Build (wk 7-12): + intervals, rucking becomes real, wall sits to 2-3min. Exit: 2h zone 2, 2min wall sit, 90s dead hang.
Phase 3 Specific (wk 13-18): back-to-back days, tempo sessions. Exit: 3h sierra ruck 15kg, fine next morning, repeat Sunday.
Phase 4 Transition (bike arrives): riding replaces 1-2 zone 2 sessions; ride length +10-15%/week, no jumps; arm pump ends first trail days hours before legs do -- normal, fades in 4-6 weeks; parking-lot drills weekly; strength drops to 2x maintenance.
Phase 5 Season (steady state): 2-3 rides/week (one long), 2 short strength sessions, mobility daily. Winter = rebuild base, same plan again.

## Skills (fitness delays collapse of technique; it doesn't substitute)

- Intro off-road course before first real trail day (Cordoba has real enduro culture).
- Off-road boots before trails. Ankle/leg injuries are the most common off-road class; boots are the cheapest prevention.
- Parking-lot slow drills weekly: tight circles, figure-8s, clutch control, braking. These double as the throttle-snatch verification protocol.
- Practice picking the bike up. 220kg dropped in sand is a legs-and-technique lift. It WILL happen.
