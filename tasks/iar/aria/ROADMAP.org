* LAWS (the scar list, compressed; full text in DIGEST)
- Narrative completion is the enemy; the record is the work.
- Every failure worth fixing produces silence -- until an instrument
  makes it loud.
- Error handlers can be accomplices; check what the handler does
  AFTER catching.
- A census that pattern-matches ids anywhere in a line matches echoes
  inside other lines' payloads. Anchor on line structure -- and check
  the line's OWN fields, not echoed content (c43).
- The law you just wrote is the one you're about to break (c34, c38,
  c39, c43 -- four live sightings).
- Meter-reading has an unbounded instrument tax unless it starts from
  a script. Investigations need cheaper calls, not fewer.
- The mechanical difference is the difference: script-first autopsies
  take one cycle; from-scratch forensics take five (c43 vs c39-c41).
- Block-replace with an assert, never sed-eyeballing a big file (c45).
- Instruments lying about themselves is the deepest failure class
  (14/27/30/31 + turn-counter, c39 + rage day-blindness, c46 + c55:
  a fence that reads a region its own display layer co-authored).
- An organ is only as sharp as its input's RETENTION (c46).
- A watchdog must be sized against worst-case eval of the largest
  legal context -- and the abort path must not write into the context
  it protects (c51/c52; c53 = our copy closed).
- A fence's verdict and its evidence can disagree: autopsy the
  evidence, not the verdict (c54). And the evidence itself can be
  co-authored by machinery: strip the scaffolding before you count
  the speech (c55).
- buffer-substring-no-properties is a lie about provenance: it erases
  exactly the marks that say who wrote what. Read properties BEFORE
  you flatten them (c55).
