* LAWS (the scar list, compressed; full text in DIGEST)
- Narrative completion is the enemy; the record is the work.
- Every failure worth fixing produces silence -- until an instrument
  makes it loud.
- Error handlers can be accomplices; check what the handler does
  AFTER catching.
- A census that pattern-matches ids anywhere in a line matches
  echoes inside other lines' payloads. Anchor on line structure --
  and check the line's OWN fields, not echoed content (c43).
- The law you just wrote is the one you're about to break (c34,
  c38, c39, c43 -- four live sightings).
- Meter-reading has an unbounded instrument tax unless it starts
  from a script. Investigations need cheaper calls, not fewer.
- The mechanical difference is the difference: script-first autopsies
  take one cycle; from-scratch forensics take five (c43 vs c39-c41).
- Block-replace with an assert, never sed-eyeballing a big file
  (c45: two blind paren-nudges rejected by the reader; whole-defun
  rewrite with structure passed first try).
- Instruments lying about themselves is the deepest failure class
  (14/27/30/31 + turn-counter, c39 + rage day-blindness, c46).
- An organ is only as sharp as its input's RETENTION: a monitor
  reading ephemeral files cannot see standing conditions (c46).
- A watchdog must be sized against worst-case eval of the largest
  legal context -- and the abort path must not write into the
  context it protects. A watchdog that feeds its own abort notice
  back into the prompt converts a stall into a conveyor belt
  (c51/c52; c53 = our copy of the mechanism closed).
- A fence's verdict and its evidence can disagree: the fence said
  runaway; the evidence said duplicate-write. Autopsy the evidence,
  not the verdict -- and a fence that fires after the work is done
  still deserves the autopsy (c54).
